The incident response runbook at Trevor Tan Incorporated is a structured operational framework designed to ensure rapid, coordinated, and effective response to any production incident affecting infrastructure, machine learning systems, or customer facing services, and the incident response process is built around the principles of minimizing customer impact, restoring system functionality as quickly as possible, and performing thorough post incident analysis to prevent recurrence, and incidents are classified into severity levels ranging from SEV0 to SEV3 where SEV0 represents a critical system outage affecting a large portion of production customers or core infrastructure, SEV1 represents major service degradation with significant customer impact, SEV2 represents partial degradation or non critical system failures, and SEV3 represents minor issues or low impact anomalies, and when an incident is detected it may be triggered either automatically through monitoring systems such as Pulse or manually reported by engineers, customers, or internal stakeholders, and once detected an incident commander is assigned who is responsible for coordinating the response effort, and the incident commander role is separate from technical remediation roles and focuses on communication, prioritization, and decision making, while subject matter experts are engaged to investigate and resolve the technical root cause, and the first step in incident response is triage where the severity, scope, and impact of the issue are assessed to determine appropriate escalation paths and response teams, and once triage is complete containment actions are taken to prevent further damage which may include rolling back recent deployments, disabling affected services, throttling traffic, or isolating problematic system components, and during this phase communication is critical and status updates are provided to stakeholders including engineering teams, product managers, leadership, and in some cases external customers depending on impact level, and after containment the investigation phase begins where engineers perform root cause analysis using logs, metrics, traces, and system diagnostics to identify the underlying cause of the incident, and this process often involves analyzing recent deployments, configuration changes, infrastructure events, or external dependencies that may have contributed to the failure, and once the root cause is identified remediation steps are implemented which may include code fixes, configuration changes, infrastructure adjustments, or data corrections depending on the nature of the issue, and after immediate resolution the system enters a recovery phase where normal operations are restored and monitoring is intensified to ensure stability, and following incident resolution a mandatory postmortem is conducted for all SEV0 and SEV1 incidents and optionally for SEV2 incidents depending on impact, and the postmortem process is blameless and focuses on identifying systemic issues rather than individual responsibility, and the postmortem document includes a detailed timeline of events, detection method analysis, impact assessment, root cause explanation, contributing factors, mitigation steps taken, and long term preventive actions, and these postmortems are reviewed by engineering leadership and shared across the organization to promote learning and continuous improvement, and preventive actions resulting from postmortems may include code changes, improved monitoring, better alerting thresholds, architectural redesigns, or updated operational procedures, and the incident response system also includes a 24/7 on call rotation where engineers are assigned responsibility for responding to production incidents during defined time windows ensuring global coverage across time zones, and communication during incidents is structured using dedicated incident channels where updates are logged in real time and stakeholders can follow progress transparently, and overall the incident response runbook at TTI is designed to ensure operational resilience, rapid recovery, and continuous learning from system failures while maintaining high availability standards for enterprise machine learning infrastructure and services.
